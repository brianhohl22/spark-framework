## 1. The organizing idea

The site has three connected parts. They serve every visitor all year:

1. **What exists:** SUSD's programs (Our programs).
2. **What's coming:** ideas and concepts under study (What we're working on).
3. **What's new:** share an idea (Share an idea). This is for ideas not already offered or being studied, and that belong with SPARK rather than another SUSD channel.

Visitors move between the parts. Someone with an idea may find it already exists, and become a supporter or a family that enrolls. A family exploring programs cares about what exists and about what's coming. So every page links to the other two parts.

Seasons such as open enrollment change how many people visit, and which dates are current. They don't change the layout.

### 1.1 What the site shows about ideas

Each level of an idea's journey shows a little more. Nothing is shown about a person, ever.

| Level | What the public sees | What stays private |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every idea, as soon as it's sent | The submitter can check their own idea's status at any time (W15) | The idea's text, the submitter's name and email, any school it names |
| All ideas this school year | Counts by theme, and how many got each kind of reply, updated monthly (W13). Short, neutral topic lines by theme (W17) | Location or school breakdowns. Those stay in the SPARK team's internal portal |
| Ideas selected for study, or later | A public title and summary written by the SPARK team, the phase, the target year, and related programs (W6) | Who sent it; support notes and comments |
| Ideas decided | The decision and a one-page summary, and "You said, we did" | The same |

**Support and comments:**
- Support is positive only; there is no "against" button.
- Suggestions, questions, and concerns about an idea being studied, and suggestions about a program, go through SUSD's existing channel, Let's Talk, under a "SPARK" topic. That topic asks which idea or program the comment is about, so each comment stays tied to it (W14). Comments are never posted, and are summarized in "You said, we did". Questions about a program go to the school.
- Support counts are not shown per idea. Whether to show them by theme is decision D17.

### 2.2 Design rules drawn from the visitors

1. **One layout all year.** Nothing is rearranged for one season. Dated content (key dates, phases, "We are here") keeps the site current.
2. **Every page links to the other two parts,** so any visitor can move around the loop in one tap.
3. **Route explorers to schools for contact and visits.** SPARK doesn't field program questions. That keeps answers fast and contact details from going stale. Suggestions about a program can go to the SPARK topic on Let's Talk.
4. **Check first, then share.** New ideas come after "Is it already offered? Is it being studied?", which is already on the Share page.
5. **Give every dated fact an owner and a refresh cadence** (HANDOFF.md, section 5).

### 2.4 Journey checks (acceptance for v3.2)

Each journey should take no more than two taps between parts, on a phone:

1. **An idea that already exists:** Home, Share an idea, "Already offered", then the theme panel on Our programs.
2. **An idea being studied:** Share an idea, "Being studied now", then the support block on What we're working on.
3. **A family exploring what's coming:** Our programs, the "not offered yet" note, then the study card and its phase.
4. **A family ready to visit:** Our programs, the theme panel, then Contact and visits and the school's tour page.
5. **A supporter checking progress:** Home, "Being studied this year" (showing Now), then the study card.
6. **A principal explaining SPARK:** How SPARK works, "What SPARK covers", then Our programs or Share an idea.
7. **A submitter checking status:** Share an idea, "Check your idea's status", enter the ID and email, then an email arrives.

### 8.2 One vocabulary for stages

The portal draft uses Needs review, Under review (committee review), and Completed. The public site and the reply emails use the stages below.
- Each internal status should map to exactly one public stage.
- The five screening outcomes need their own field or status, because the public counts and the emails depend on them.
- Every idea needs exactly one status at all times, so the portal's counts add up to the total.

| Portal status (draft) | Public stage | What the submitter is told |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs review | Received | Confirmation, with the idea ID and a reply-by date |
| (screening done) | Screened, with one of five outcomes | The outcome and the reason |
| Under review (committee review) | SPARK team review | The review date |
| Completed | Selected for study (shown on the board); Develop further or Archived (emails only, not on the board); later, Decided or Implementing (shown) | The result and what's next |

### 8.3 Status check method (D19)

**Recommended: a small Microsoft Form plus a flow,** using standard connectors that are included with Microsoft 365: Forms, SharePoint, and Office 365 Outlook. District IT confirms that Power Automate and the Forms setting "Anyone can respond" are on.

1. A submitter enters their idea ID and email in the "Check your idea's status" form (W15).
2. The flow finds the idea by its ID. It sends an email only if the ID and the email both match the stored record, ignoring capital letters and spaces. The email goes to the stored address, with the current public stage, outcome reason, and next step.
3. It sends at most one status email per idea per day.
4. **Merged ideas.** For an idea joined to another, the email gives the idea's own outcome, plus the other idea's public stage and next step. It quotes the other idea's title or reason only if that idea is already published.
5. The form shows the same neutral thank-you message every time, whether or not anything matched.
6. The flow sends from the SPARK shared mailbox, not a person's account. Check that outside-recipient rules allow mail from it.

**Not recommended:**
- **Opening the list itself to the public.** SharePoint can't share a whole list anonymously, admins may turn off "Anyone" links, and the IDs run in sequence, so they are easy to guess.
- **A Power Pages site.** It needs Power Pages capacity (for example, a $75-a-month pack at list price, or pay-as-you-go through Azure) plus setup. That's too much for a low-volume lookup.

**The Web Content team decides the final method.** The idea ID is a reference number, not a password.

### 8.4 Counts and refresh

- **What gets counted:** the public counts (W13, W17) are a subset of the internal dashboard: by theme and by outcome, never by location. The counting rules are in W13.
- **Source of truth:** a monthly summary generated on the 1st from the SPARK list. Its date is the "As of" date on the page.
- **Getting it onto the page:** Finalsite's help center documents no SharePoint feed. Power BI's "Publish to web" would make the report and its data public, with no row-level security, and needs a tenant setting. So the Web Content team updates a static table each month (steady-state items S3 and S16).

### 8.5 Privacy rules the public site relies on

- Names, emails, and school or location details never leave the list.
- An idea gets a public title only once it is selected for study.
- Comments and support notes are never published. No support numbers appear on the site (D17 default).
- Counts are shown by theme only. Location breakdowns stay in the internal portal, because they could point to specific schools.
- Topic lines never name a school, site, group, or person.
