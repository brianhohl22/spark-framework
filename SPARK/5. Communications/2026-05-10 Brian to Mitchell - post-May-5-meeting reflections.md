First, in the lead-up to the Echo decision, I noticed other parents referring to "the district" almost as an inanimate object — Dr. Menzel was the positive symbol, but there was a mystery about many of the others working behind the scenes, and I think that gap created some unfair characterizations.

It's not that I was 'surprised' by this.  I just wonder if there's an opportunity to spotlight team members — a brief monthly note or video sharing what's on their mind, what they worry about, or what they're hopeful for. Or a district team member joining something that already happens (e.g., Echo's monthly parent-admin coffee chat after the family read), both as themselves in their district role and as themselves outside it.

Probably already a thing, and I just need to pay better attention!  But I think other parents would love to know what I now understand better about the team.

One small "wondering" (with my past life consulting hat on):

The competitive comparison table was so cool, and it's clearly valuable to be the only school checking every box.  If some of those comparison schools are market leaders, they're presumably "successful" — which had me wondering: for each box we check that they don't, did they (a) consider and reject it (e.g. given cost of doing it all?), (b) try it and stop (e.g. because of not enough demand?), or (c) never explore it?

## On the Gifted & Advanced Studies Hub

I also love this concept.  If it existed, I think we'd consider it for our older daughter!
Just a few thoughts (mostly related to questions and thoughts from others in the meeting):

**1. Alpha School as a model reference.** Someone asked about the cost of learning guides — I can imagine that (semi-)personalization is challenging at scale. I wanted to share Alpha School as one reference. You probably already know about them!  They're not gifted-focused (no entry testing — different population), but the model is similar: mastery/progress-based core learning in the morning, life-skills-type projects and challenges in the afternoon.

They're trying to solve the personalization-at-scale thing with technology in the morning core lessons. I believe they recently opened a branch in Scottsdale, so they may also be a useful local reference.

They are a private school with a different structure, so not directly comparable, but their tech-enabled mastery-type learning might be worth glancing at if cost at scale might be an issue.  With the crazy fast advance of AI-led tools these days, and maybe building on IXL and Boost-type tools, creating tech-supporting instruction may be possible at lower cost than in the past?

**2. Estimating enrollment (the inside-vs-outside SUSD question Dr. Menzel raised).** Given the district's experience with new programs, you probably have experts on the district team with a ton of experience on this sort of thing.  So please ignore if annoying / not useful!  Sharing a few thoughts - flashing back to my consulting days again - in case useful…

It might be interesting to sort of 'triangulate' across a few sources, each from a different 'angle':

- Internal benchmarks — past SUSD specialty launches (e.g. MSA, DLI, IB) and what their inside-vs-outside break-down looked like in the first few years
- External but similar and close — charter and private specialty openings near SUSD in recent years and any outflow they caused. (Alpha's Scottsdale branch might also be considered a data point / demand signal for unique K–8 in Scottsdale -- small but growing fast from my understanding)
- Surveys — maybe trying to do a few over time is helpful; trying to figure out the gap between "we would consider this" and "we would commit to this!" in the survey structure (easier said than done!);. Then, as marketing rolls out, it might be interesting to see if survey responses shift over time

The three together could give a range of demand and might help for sort of planning for the 'boundaries'…

## A few other (random?) thoughts

- **Pre-trial the methodology in an existing CGP school first?** If the gifted academy is not on track for a Fall 2026 launch, could it be a chance for a small pilot / trial run?  I wonder if possible / useful to pilot the approach — or one component, like mastery-based progression — in an existing CGP setting (Kiva, Redfield, Tavan, etc.). The team might even hire or dual-hat an existing gifted teacher into the learning-guide role, so by launch time there would already be team member(s) who have run the model. Side benefit: you might be able to get some useful pre-launch feedback from students and even parents on the concept

- **Could the two academies share a site to start?** If budget or enrollment is a concern for either or both, could co-locating the two at one site (Echo Canyon, Pima, or any underutilized campus) make it an easier sell for the board and community?  Perhaps the aim would be to split them once one or both are proving themselves, but… could phase the facility changes, starting with reversible or universally-useful updates; program-specific build-outs could be saved for when enrollment numbers are up and once e.g. one moves to another site. Cross-over learning opportunities might actually be really cool and valuable — arts focus might be thought of as having a gifted-friendly approach, and gifted-style mastery approach could make the arts education even stronger. Of course there are reasons this does not make sense (brand identity, scheduling, space, disruption in moving if they do split eventually), but just a thought!

- **Is the gifted academy teaching methodology interesting beyond gifted?** The mastery-based core block + afternoon enrichment/exploration format sounds like a really cool fit at a gifted academy...

  I wonder -- if SUSD pilots it for gifted and it works, would the district have an option to run that format in other places — for any students who might thrive there? Tech might matter even more in that case (assuming it enables scale), but if the belief is that (semi-)personalization of core + 'Exploration Friday'-type growth opportunities can help improve outcomes and prepare kids for successful futures, could the pattern itself be extendable beyond gifted?  (again I wonder if Alpha School (and maybe others like it?) could be an interesting market study on this topic)
